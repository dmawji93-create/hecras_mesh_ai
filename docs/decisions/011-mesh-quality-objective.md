# ADR 011: Quantitative Mesh-Quality Objective

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-05-23

## Context

The project's value proposition has two halves: hand-built meshes are slow, and hand-built meshes are imperfect — bounded by expert intuition, inconsistent between modelers, unauditable. Solving only the speed problem (by imitating experts) leaves the quality problem untouched and inherits a hard ceiling: a model trained to reproduce expert meshes can at best asymptote to the average expert and inherits their blind spots.

To solve both problems, the project must *optimize* mesh quality rather than *imitate* it. Optimization requires an objective. Today "mesh quality" is tacit knowledge — it lives in experts' heads, is not expressed as a number, and cannot be compared, ranked, or systematically improved. ADR 003's "performance fine-tune" stage is undefined until this objective exists.

This ADR defines mesh quality as a quantitative objective. You cannot optimize, automate, or audit what you cannot measure; making mesh quality a number is the foundational move from which the rest of the project follows.

## Decision

Define the quality of a candidate mesh **M** for a modeling case **c** as a quantitative objective with three components: a **reference solution**, an **error functional**, and a **cost term**.

### 1. Reference solution (numerical truth)

For each training and evaluation case, establish a converged, mesh-independent reference solution:

- Run a grid-refinement sequence (successively finer meshes on the same case).
- Demonstrate convergence using Richardson extrapolation and the Grid Convergence Index (GCI, Roache's method).
- The reference is the solution at the resolution where the GCI relative to the next-finer mesh is within tolerance.
- Where full convergence is computationally infeasible (large domains), use the finest practical mesh and record the residual GCI as an explicit, reported uncertainty.

The reference is *numerical truth* — the answer the governing equations give in the limit of vanishing discretization error.

### 2. Error functional `E(M, c)` — purpose-dependent

The error of a candidate mesh is measured against the reference solution. Critically, **the error functional is purpose-dependent and must be a declared input**, not a fixed assumption — the mesh that minimizes peak-stage error is not the mesh that minimizes inundation-extent error or arrival-time error.

Candidate error components (selectable and weightable per study purpose):

- **Peak water-surface error** — spatial RMSE (or maximum) of `|WSE_M - WSE_ref|` at peak, optionally restricted to or weighted toward a declared area of interest.
- **Inundation-extent error** — wet/dry agreement against the reference, scored by Critical Success Index / IoU of the inundation polygon.
- **Timing error** — error in time-to-peak or in flood-front arrival time.
- **Velocity-field error** — error in peak velocity, for scour, structure, and ecological applications.
- **Mass-conservation error** — volume residual; primarily a sanity and disqualification metric.

The default functional, absent a stated purpose, is a general flood-mapping combination (peak WSE + inundation extent).

### 3. Cost term `C(M, c)`

Mesh quality is intrinsically a tradeoff against compute — refining cells forces smaller timesteps via the Courant condition. The cost term:

- **Cell count** — the primary, hardware-independent, reproducible signal used in training.
- **Wall-clock runtime** — recorded alongside for reporting and user-facing decisions.

### Objective form

The objective is fundamentally **Pareto** — error versus cost. The project will (a) trace the Pareto front where feasible, and (b) for single-mesh proposals support both a scalarized form `J = E + lambda * C` and a constrained form `minimize E subject to C <= budget`. The choice of form, and of `lambda` or the budget, is a per-use-case input.

### Physical anchoring

The reference solution is numerical truth, not physical truth — it is still a model with its own structural error against reality. Where physical observations exist (stream gauges, high-water marks, recorded inundation extents), the methodology incorporates them as a validation gate or an additional error term, so that optimization toward numerical truth cannot silently drift away from physical truth.

## Consequences

### Positive
- Mesh quality becomes **measurable** — the prerequisite for optimizing, automating, and auditing it.
- Removes the expert-imitation ceiling: the model can in principle exceed any individual expert on the declared objective.
- Gives ADR 003's fine-tune stage and the Phase C closed loop a well-defined reward.
- Produces **defensible** meshes — "generated by a documented procedure minimizing X against a converged reference" is a far stronger regulatory statement than "an experienced modeler drew it."
- Purpose-dependence is exposed as a feature: the tool can target the study's actual goal.

### Negative / risks
- **Expensive.** Requires a grid-refinement sequence — multiple runs, including very fine ones — for every reference case. This is the dominant compute cost of the project and directly shapes the deferred compute-target decision.
- **Garbage-objective risk.** A poorly chosen error functional yields poorly "optimal" meshes; the functional itself must be validated.
- **Reference-artifact risk.** Optimizing purely toward a fine-mesh solution can perfect a numerical artifact; mitigated by physical anchoring.
- **Convergence is not guaranteed.** SWE solutions with shocks, hydraulic jumps, and wet/dry fronts may not show clean monotone grid convergence; GCI can be ill-behaved and needs careful, possibly case-specific handling.
- Purpose-dependence means there is no single universal "best mesh," adding product and UX complexity.

## Alternatives considered

- **Expert meshes as ground truth** (ADR 003's original framing): rejected as the *target* — caps quality at expert level. Retained as a *prior* — see the ADR 003 amendment of 2026-05-23.
- **Solution-gradient indicators without a reference solution:** cheaper (no fine run needed), but gradient/curvature indicators reveal where error *concentrates*, not its *magnitude* — they cannot rank two whole meshes or certify accuracy. Useful as a *component* of the system and as a Phase C refinement signal, not as the objective.
- **Observation-only objective:** anchored to physical truth but far too sparse — most domains have few or no gauges, and high-water marks are event-specific.
- **Geometric mesh-quality scores** (skewness, aspect ratio, orthogonality — classic CFD metrics): measure geometric regularity, not solution accuracy. Necessary but not sufficient; may be retained as disqualification constraints.

## Open questions

- Exact components and weights of the default error functional.
- Scalarized vs constrained vs full-Pareto treatment for the model's training signal.
- How to make reference runs affordable at corpus scale — subsample the corpus, restrict refinement sequences, or substitute a learned surrogate for the finest levels.
- Handling of cases where mesh convergence cannot be cleanly established.
- Whether geometric quality scores enter as hard constraints or soft penalties.
